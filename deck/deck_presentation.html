Slide 1 of 5 • The Question

The "Time Machine" Credit Model

How Fast Does a Loan-Approval Model Decay, and What Warns You First?

  • The Core Dilemma: Commercial banks operate credit scorecards subject to Model Risk Governance (SR 11-7), but applicant populations, economic cycles, and risk profiles continuously drift.
  • The Research Question: In an out-of-time production environment, how many months or years does a credit scorecard remain viable before discrimination, calibration, and profitability decay?
  • The "Time Machine" Methodology: We fit a standardized WOE logistic scorecard strictly on 2012–2014 originations, freeze its parameters, and test it prospectively across 2015–2018.
Slide 2 of 5 • Data & Time Split

Regulatory Out-of-Time Discipline

Preventing Data Leakage and Look-Ahead Bias

  • No Random Splits: Shuffling multi-year lending records allows models to peek into future economic regimes, creating overly optimistic performance estimates.
  • Cohort Isolation: 36-month completed Lending Club loans (bad = 1 if Charged Off, 0 if Fully Paid). Active or unfinished loans are excluded.
  • Zero Model Leakage: Post-origination performance metrics and Lending Club's proprietary model outputs (grade, sub_grade, int_rate) are purged to isolate true fundamental borrower risk.
  • Time Split Architecture:
    • Build (2012-2014) Training, WOE binning, IV filtering, scorecard scaling.
    • Validate (2015) In-sample benchmark; establish the 70% approval cut-off (533 points).
    • Test (2016-2018) Out-of-time longitudinal evaluation of the frozen model.

Slide 4 of 5 • Surveillance & Lead Time

Early-Warning Surveillance Signals

Population Stability (PSI) & Covariate Drift (CSI)

  • Surveillance Engine: Decoupling input demographic shift from lagging loan default outcomes.
    • PSI on Scores: Measured against the 2012–2014 baseline decile distribution (<0.10 stable, 0.10–0.25 monitor, >0.25 trigger).
    • CSI on Predictors: Characteristic stability index isolates which specific variable shifted first (DTI and inquiries led the drift).
  • Alert Criteria: PSI > 0.25 OR |Calibration Gap| > 0.25.
  • Key Surveillance Finding: Calibration and covariate alerts provided a 1 to 2 year early-warning lead time before any breakdown in score ranking power occurred.
Slide 5 of 5 • Remediation & Recommendation

Remediation Policies & Monitoring Plan

Comparing Strategy A, B, and C Under Real Portfolio Economics

  • Strategy A (Do Nothing): Approval volume dropped as the applicant pool drifted, sacrificing portfolio growth ($65.8\% - 68.3\%$).
  • Strategy B (Reset Cut-off): Re-benchmarking the cut-off annually restored target 70% approval and captured over 95% of rebuild value with zero model retraining costs.
  • Strategy C (Model Rebuild): Rolling 3-year retrained scorecards yielded minimal Gini gains while incurring significant governance validation friction.
  • Executive Recommendation:
    1. Adopt annual cut-off recalibration (Strategy B) as standard operational policy.
    2. Trigger full model rebuilds (Strategy C) strictly when both PSI > 0.25 and Gini degradation exceeds 5 points.
    3. Maintain continuous quarterly CSI monitoring to identify shifting applicant risk tiers early.
